Papaver rhoeas

Common Name(s):

  • Corn Poppy
  • Field Poppy
  • Flanders Poppy
  • Red Poppy

Previously known as:

  • Papaver arvaticum
  • Papaver vulgaris
Phonetic Spelling
puh-PAY-ver ROH-ee-as
Description

Corn Poppy is an annual herbaceous species of flowering plant in the poppy family native to Europe and Asia. Numerous red blooms appear spring and summer and again in the fall, but each individual bloom lasts only a day. Cultivars are available in white and purple. Corn Poppy is the common field poppy of Europe and became a symbol for the losses incurred on the battlefields of World War I.The genus name comes from the Latin for poppy. The species name is Latin for red in reference to the flower color.

Corn Poppy is best grown in fertile, medium moist, well-drained soils in full sun. It does well in cool summer climates with some afternoon shade where summers tend to be hot. Good drainage is important. It is an annual, but vigorously self seeds to maintain its presence in the garden year after year.

Use in a non-native naturalized area or as groups in border fronts and sow seeds successfully for a longer bloom season. Corn Poppy can easily escape from cultivation and has been described as weedy. The seeds can remain vital for a long time, known to have sprouted after 80 years of dormancy. 

Clemson University has an article on corn poppies in its Home and Garden Information Center.

Diseases, Insect Pests, and Other Plant Problems:

No known insect or disease problems. Wet soils can cause root rot. Botrytis and powdery mildew can also occur.
